# State of the Art: LoL / Esports Win Prediction Modeling

Research pass for rift-quant. Compiled 2026-08-21. All web sources accessed 2026-08-21 unless noted. Confidence tags: **High** (corroborated by primary source and/or multiple converging sources), **Med** (single credible source, plausible, or partially verified), **Low** (single blog/community source, unverifiable extraction, or dated data) — treat Low-confidence numbers as directional only, not load-bearing.

A methodological warning that recurs constantly in this space and applies to almost every "accuracy" number below: **published numbers are rarely apples-to-apples.** They differ on (a) pro vs. solo-queue vs. mixed-rank data, (b) pure draft vs. draft+player-identity vs. draft+mastery features, (c) train/test split by time vs. randomly (random splits leak future patches/rosters and inflate accuracy), and (d) accuracy vs. log-loss vs. AUC vs. calibration error as the reported metric. rift-quant should define one internal eval protocol before comparing itself to any external claim.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Pre-game/draft-only ceiling is low and team-quality-dominated.** One pro-focused analysis estimates bookmakers hit ~63% accuracy *before draft even starts* (team strength alone), and that draft itself only marginally shifts outcomes (~10% informal estimate) relative to team quality, day-of form, and variance. Draft-only academic models land in the 55–74% range depending on data/skill tier — with the highest numbers (Dota, high-skill solo queue) not transferring cleanly to pro play. **[Med]**
- **In-game accuracy follows a consistent S-curve** across every source found: ~50% at draft → high-50s/60s% by ~10 min → 65–75% by 15–20 min (gold-diff-at-15 becomes a strong signal) → 75–85% by ~25 min → 90%+ by 30–35 min → asymptotic near-certainty by 40+ min. Exact numbers vary by dataset, but the *shape* is remarkably consistent across LoL, Dota 2, and Honor of Kings sources. **[High on shape, Med on exact numbers]**
- **Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost/Extra Trees/Random Forest) are the practical default** for tabular in-game/end-game state across nearly every paper and production system found (PandaSkill's per-role performance model, Dota outcome-prediction papers, match-fixing detection). **Logistic regression remains the industry workhorse** for live win-probability specifically (Oracle's Elixir's production model, the GD@15 "Early-Game Rating" model, academic role-specific regressions) because it's fast, interpretable, and easy to calibrate/deploy live. **[High]**
- **LSTM/Transformer sequence models help most when fed genuine sequential/positional data** (CS2 tick-level positions, VALORANT tactical video features), not when fed the same periodic aggregate snapshots (gold/xp/kills) that GBMs already handle well. Evidence that sequence models beat GBM on standard tabular MOBA snapshots is thin. **[Med]**
- **PandaSkill (2025) is the single most directly-applicable paper found.** It builds per-role XGBoost performance scores from box-score stats, converts them to percentiles, and feeds them into a modified OpenSkill (Bayesian, Plackett-Luce) update that ranks players individually within a match rather than just crediting team W/L. It explicitly splits each player's rating into a **contextual (in-region) component and a meta (region-strength) component** — precisely the LCK-vs-LPL-vs-LEC calibration problem rift-quant needs solved — and reports 64.98–70.07% pre-match accuracy depending on variant, rising to ~67–70% specifically on **inter-region** matches (Worlds/MSI-type games). **[High — html full text verified]**
- **Blue side advantage is real but has been shrinking and is now regime-dependent.** Aggregate ~51.9% blue win rate across majors since 2020, trending down (52.1% → 51.8% → 51.5% by year in one dataset), but Worlds 2025 was ~50/50 tournament-wide while LCK specifically won 58.3% of its games on blue side — suggesting the edge is now more about which team best *exploits* first pick than a fixed structural constant. **[Med]**
- **2026 is a regime-change year for this exact feature.** Riot introduced a "First Selection" system for the 2026 season that decouples map side from draft pick-order priority (previously bundled together), explicitly to neutralize the blue-side pick-order edge. Any blue-side feature trained on pre-2026 data will not directly transfer to 2026+ matches. **[High — 8+ converging esports-press sources]**
- **Fearless draft (banning previously-used champions for the rest of a series, LPL first in 2024, expanded across majors through 2025) changes draft-phase dynamics** by shrinking available counter-picks as a series progresses, and several outlets explicitly flag it as compounding the blue-side pick-order advantage over a Bo3/Bo5. **[Med]**
- **Champion synergy/counter modeling has moved from hand-built matrices to learned embeddings**: "Game Avatar Embedding" (2018) pioneered pairwise synergy+opposition vectors from match outcomes; DraftRec (WWW'22, official code available) uses a hierarchical transformer over player history + match context; a 2026 paper on player-conditional champion ranking explicitly tackles small-sample pro pools via **mastery-prior shrinkage** and **archetype-constrained discovery** — exactly the small-pro-sample problem rift-quant faces. **[Med-High]**
- **Solo-queue data does NOT transfer 1:1 to pro play**, and the failure mode is champion-specific, not a single global correction. One analysis finds per-champion "pro multipliers" (e.g., Corki overperforms pro-vs-SQ by ~1.19x) driven by three factors: skill-ceiling (mechanically demanding champs scale up), coordination-dependence (surprise/assassin picks underperform once comms neutralize them; coordinated-execution picks overperform), and strategic-execution requirements. Recommended minimum ~2,000 games per champion for a reliable SQ win-rate signal. **[Med]**
- **Momentum/tilt is genuinely contested science, not settled fact — in any sport.** General sports analytics shows mixed hot-hand evidence; the one directly-relevant esports paper found (psychological momentum forecasting) claims a learned momentum representation improves draft-phase prediction, but full-text verification of effect size was not possible in this pass. Treat as a feature hypothesis to backtest, not an assumed prior. **[Low-Med]**
- **A LoL-specific "deserved-to-win"/xG-style metric and a genuine (non-fraud) "throw/collapse detector" do not appear to exist publicly.** Win-Probability-Added (WPA) is well-established in traditional sports and has a live production analog in CS2 (HLTV Rating 3.0's "Round Swing"), and one academic LoL regression already does coarse per-event attribution (baron = +24pp, ADC kill = +5.9pp, blue side = +7pp). But nobody has published a LoL "collapsed a >90% win probability" detector — this is whitespace rift-quant could own. **[Med on WPA transfer, Low on any existing throw-detector]**

## 2. Detailed Findings

### 2.1 Draft-only / pre-game prediction accuracy (pro LoL) — realistic ceiling

The strongest single anchor found is from iTero.gg's professional-play draft analysis: **"bookies can predict the result of a game with a roughly 63% accuracy before they even walk on stage"** (i.e., team-strength-only, pre-draft), and separately, **"roughly 10% of games are influenced by draft"**, with team quality, day-of performance, and random luck accounting for the rest. The same author, after building a full draft model incorporating player-on-champion history, early gold-lead-by-matchup patterns, high-elo lane-counter data, and AI-generated tier lists, states plainly: *"I have not solved drafting... that alpha does not yet consistently exceed the cut the house takes."* [Med] — [LoL: How to Win Draft in Pro Play, itero.gg](https://www.itero.gg/articles/draft-pro), accessed 2026-08-21.

Vendor/production tools:
- **LoLDraftAI**: solo-queue model overall accuracy **56%**, but well-calibrated (bucketed: at 70–72% predicted win rate, actual observed rate is 72.54% vs. 71.25% expected — i.e. calibration is tighter than raw accuracy suggests). Only 3 raw inputs: champions played, patch, elo — all champion-level stats are learned internally rather than hand-fed. The **pro model is the solo-queue model fine-tuned on pro data**, explicitly to compensate for small pro sample size; the author flags a resulting bias risk toward lower-tier teams (who simply have more recorded games) and inflated win rates for team-specific/situational picks. Reportedly open-sourced ~May 2025. [Med] — [LoLDraftAI explained](https://loldraftai.com/blog/loldraftai-explained), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **draftlol.ai**: Bayesian model combining 7 signals — champion win rate on current patch, lane-matchup win rate, duo synergy, recent team form, head-to-head history, team Elo, and season win rate — recalculated live after every pick. No accuracy numbers disclosed on the public prediction page; the site also runs a model-vs-prediction-market comparison (relevant to rift-quant's pricing ambitions) but the comparison page itself did not yield quantitative figures on this pass. [Med] — [draftlol.ai](https://draftlol.ai/predictions), accessed 2026-08-21.

Academic (mostly Dota 2, methods transfer):
- **Kalyanaraman (2014)**: hero-role features + genetic algorithm + logistic regression → **74.1%** accuracy on high-skill-rank matches. [Med, via secondary aggregation]
- **Semenov et al. (2016)**: Naive Bayes / logistic regression / GBDT → **70.6% / 67% / 66%** accuracy across average/high/very-high skill tiers; factorization machines gave best AUC (0.706 / 0.670 / 0.660 by tier). Classic, widely-cited draft-prediction baseline. [Med]
- **MDPI 2025** ("Applications of Linear and Ensemble-Based ML for Predicting Winning Teams in LoL"): a companion 2024 study in the same space trained logistic regression / MLP / SVM / XGBoost on 10,390 pro games (2023) with an independent 9,670-game 2024 test set, using a "Victory Contribution" role-sensitive metric. Full numeric results could not be extracted from the PDF in this pass — worth a direct read. [Low — extraction failed, existence/design only]

**Interpretation for rift-quant**: A pure-draft pro-play model should realistically target **~55–62% accuracy / log-loss materially better than 0.693 but well short of in-game numbers**. Anything claiming much higher (e.g., community repos reporting 87.9%) is almost always measuring something else — see §2.8 for the specific methodological trap.

### 2.3 Methods comparison

- **GBM/tree ensembles (XGBoost, Random Forest, Extra Trees) are the default for tabular in-game/end-game state** — used by PandaSkill's per-role performance scorer, the Dota temporal-segmentation paper (91–99% by phase), and match-fixing anomaly detectors. **[High]**
- **Logistic regression remains the production/industry standard for live win-probability specifically** — Oracle's Elixir's real system, the GD@15 model, and the Samford role-based regression are all plain LR, valued for speed, interpretability, and ease of live deployment/calibration over marginal accuracy gains from more complex models. **[High]**
- **LSTM/Transformer sequence models**: found across fighting-game health-bar time series, VALORANT (Seq2Seq LSTM for second-half/momentum prediction), and CS2 (Vision Transformer on gameplay footage; graph-classification over positional ticks). The pattern across sources: sequence/deep models add value **when the input is genuinely sequential/high-resolution** (raw positions, video, tick data) — evidence that they beat GBMs on the kind of periodic aggregate snapshots (gold/xp/kills every N minutes) typically available for LoL is thin to absent in what was found. **[Med]**
- **Time-slice vs. single-model-with-time-interaction**: the dominant applied pattern (Oracle's Elixir: "minute-by-minute", one classic LoL blog approach: 13 independent 5-minute-bucket logistic models) is **independent models per time bucket**. The alternative — one model with elapsed time as a feature plus explicit interaction terms (e.g., gold_diff × spline(time), motivated by the empirically-observed sigmoid-steepens-early-flattens-late shape) — was not found explicitly benchmarked against the bucketed approach in any source, but is a natural, lower-variance alternative that avoids discontinuities at bucket boundaries and pools statistical power across a data-scarce pro dataset. **[Synthesis — flagged as our own reasoning, not a cited finding]**
- **Calibration methods**: standard ML literature draws a clean line — **Platt scaling** (fit a logistic curve to raw scores) is parametric, sigmoid-shaped, and data-efficient, good for small calibration sets or naturally sigmoid-miscalibrated models (SVM-like). **Isotonic regression** is non-parametric/monotonic, more flexible, but needs roughly **1,000+ calibration points** to reliably match-or-beat Platt. **[High, generic ML result]** — [Train In Data calibration overview](https://www.blog.trainindata.com/probability-calibration-in-machine-learning/); [Betting-specific calibration overview](https://www.underdogchance.com/betting-model-calibration-techniques/), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **LoL-specific calibration work exists**: "A Confidence-Calibrated MOBA Game Winner Predictor" (IEEE CoG 2020) proposes a data-uncertainty-aware calibration method for LoL win prediction, reporting **Expected Calibration Error of 0.57%** vs. **1.11%** for standard temperature scaling — i.e., roughly halving calibration error over a common generic baseline. **[Med — single extraction, plausible and specific]**
- **Why calibration matters for betting/pricing**: this is well-established generically (Brier score / log-loss / ECE as the standard evaluation trio) — a model that is directionally accurate but poorly calibrated at the extremes (e.g., says 95% when true rate is 85%) is exactly where a market maker gets picked off, since extreme probabilities are where bet/contract sizing is most sensitive to small calibration errors. **[High, generic result, directly applicable]**

### 2.4 Team/player rating systems

- **Elo**: two-player design, doesn't natively decompose team results into individual contribution; needs an extension for 5v5. **[High, background fact]**
- **Glicko-2** (Glickman): adds rating deviation (uncertainty) and volatility on top of Elo; a 2025 IEEE review notes Glicko has been used for football and Dota 2 team rankings but shares Elo's two-player-native limitation for team games (doesn't decompose individual contribution within a team result). **[Med]** — ["From Ratings to Balance: A Review of Glicko's Role in Competitive Gaming Analytics"](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10959302), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **TrueSkill** (Microsoft, Bayesian, proprietary) and **OpenSkill** (open re-implementation using a Plackett-Luce model) are both designed natively for multi-team, multi-player, asymmetric matches. OpenSkill's own paper claims faster computation and comparable-or-better predictive accuracy than TrueSkill. **[High]** — [OpenSkill paper, arXiv 2401.05451](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05451), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **PandaSkill** (arXiv 2501.10049, 2025) — **the standout, directly-applicable paper**:
  - *Performance layer*: per-role XGBoost models predict end-of-game win probability from box-score stats (KDA, gold/xp per minute, CS, damage, objective participation), converted to a 0–100 percentile "PScore."
  - *Rating layer*: feeds PScore into an OpenSkill Bayesian update, but with a key trick — treats each match as **Free-For-All**, ranking all 10 players individually by PScore rather than crediting only team-level win/loss, so a strong performance on the losing side still moves a player's rating up relative to peers.
  - *Region calibration (directly answers rift-quant's ask)*: decomposes each player's rating mean as **μ = μ_contextual + μ_meta**, where μ_contextual updates on any game and μ_meta (region strength) updates *only* on inter-region games (Worlds/MSI), with rolling 1-month validation windows to track meta shifts.
  - *Roster changes*: when a player switches regions, **reset σ_contextual to its default (25/3)** to represent renewed uncertainty, rather than resetting the mean.
  - *Data*: 37,388 pro games, 4,927 players, 8 regions (LCK/LPL/LEC/LCS/PCS/VCS/CBLOL/LLA), 5 years.
  - *Results*: pre-match logistic-regression-on-top-of-rating accuracy of **64.98–65.56%** overall across variants, rising to **67.39–70.07%** specifically on **inter-region** games — i.e., exactly the Worlds/MSI cross-region calibration case rift-quant cares about most. Expert concordance: **80.63%** majority agreement, **88.98%** unanimity agreement vs. human pro-analyst predictions. **[High — verified via full HTML text, internally consistent, specific numbers with a comparison table]** — [arXiv 2501.10049 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10049), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **SIDO performance model** (arXiv 2403.04873, 2024): hierarchical Bayesian regression decomposing a player's gold/damage output into individual, ally, and enemy random effects, computed over 3 phases (0–7 / 7–15 / 15–25 min), validated on **289,000 solo-queue games across 5,500+ accounts and 165+ champions**. Shows pro players scoring ~1 SD above average even controlling for role/meta, and separates "technical" skill (own stats) from "strategic" skill (impact on allies/enemies) — potentially a useful mechanical-skill-extraction layer feeding a team rating. **[Med-High — verified via HTML]** — [arXiv 2403.04873 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.04873v1), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Independent convergent validity**: the hobbyist site lolproelo.com implements Elo as **"Effective Elo = Home Elo + Region Strength,"** with region strength moved *only* by inter-region games at internationals — structurally identical to PandaSkill's academic contextual/meta decomposition, arrived at independently. Good sign this is a robust, converged-upon pattern rather than one paper's idiosyncratic choice. **[Med — could not fetch full methodology page directly (403/socket errors); relying on search-engine synopsis]** — [lolproelo.com](https://lolproelo.com/regions), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Current regional pecking order** (as reported, not asserted from model knowledge): LCK reported as strongest region entering the 2025 season discussion, LPL as "the only genuine challenger," LEC improving via "creative drafts" but lacking depth — per an esports-press regional-power ranking. Worlds 2025 recap sources describe LCK going 58.3% on blue side specifically at that event. **[Med — press sources, should be re-checked against actual Worlds 2025/2026 results directly in the project's own data pipeline rather than trusted long-term]** — [Esports Insider regional ranking](https://esportsinsider.com/best-league-of-legends-region-2025-ranking), accessed 2026-08-21.

### 2.5 Champion synergy/counter modeling under small pro samples

- **Game Avatar Embedding (GAE)** (arXiv 1803.10402, 2018) — the original "champ2vec"-style approach: a latent-variable model learning per-champion vectors that separately encode **synergy** (same-team pairwise) and **opposition/counter** (cross-team pairwise) relationships, trained by maximizing likelihood of observed match outcomes, validated across three commercial MOBA titles. Downstream uses shown: similar-champion search, match-outcome prediction, pick recommendation. **[High on existence/approach, abstract-level only for numbers]** — [arXiv 1803.10402](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10402), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **DraftRec** (arXiv 2204.12750, WWW 2022, **official code on GitHub**): hierarchical model with two transformer towers — a *player network* capturing individual playstyle/history and a *match network* integrating all 10 players — trained on 280,000 LoL matches + 50,000 Dota 2 matches, claimed SOTA at publication for both pick recommendation and outcome prediction, validated with a real user survey. Directly reusable as an architecture template (and literal starting code) for a personalized, small-sample-aware pick model. **[High — official repo exists]** — [github.com/dojeon-ai/DraftRec](https://github.com/dojeon-ai/DraftRec), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Graph-based champion interaction study** (SciTePress 2026): builds separate synergy and antagonist (counter) weighted graphs from 40,000 ranked games, embeds 225 champion-role nodes via **Deep Graph Infomax + GCN** (30 input features → 64-unit hidden layer → 32-dim latent embedding), and recovers three interpretable synergy archetypes via unsupervised clustering: **Front-to-Back** (protect-the-carry), **Skirmishers** (chaotic 2v2 dominance), **Dive Squad** (gap-close-and-all-in) — described as following rock-paper-scissors-style cyclic dominance. No predictive accuracy reported; the contribution is structural/interpretive rather than a win-prediction benchmark. **[Med]** — SciTePress 2026 (147388), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Robust Player-Conditional Champion Ranking** (arXiv 2605.18338, 2026 — very recent): directly targets the exact small-pro-sample problem rift-quant faces. Generates **player-specific** champion rankings (not a single global tier list) via: (1) **mastery priors** — shrinkage toward population/archetype averages when a given player has few recorded games on a champion, so estimates stay reliable under sparse data; (2) **archetype-constrained discovery** — clustering players/champions into playstyle archetypes and constraining recommendations within-archetype for robustness and interpretability. This is close to a ready-made blueprint for modeling **pro player champion pools** from limited pro-game counts by borrowing strength from the player's broader (including solo-queue) history and archetype peers. **[Med — summary-level extraction, numeric results not confirmed]** — [arXiv 2605.18338](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18338), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Solo-queue → pro transfer** is explicitly *not* a clean 1:1 mapping. iTero.gg's analysis: Gwen had a 50% solo-queue win rate but won 67% of games at MSI — "no obvious correlation" from raw SQ win rate alone. Per-champion multipliers exist instead (e.g., Corki overperforms pro-vs-SQ by **~1.19x**, so a 45% SQ win rate implies ~53.5% pro performance). Three named drivers of divergence: **skill ceiling** (high-ceiling champs like Qiyana/Jayce scale up with player skill; low-ceiling champs like Garen don't), **coordination dependence** (assassins/surprise-based picks underperform once comms neutralize the surprise; setup/follow-up-dependent picks like Twisted Fate/Azir overperform with coordinated teams), and **strategic execution requirements** (win conditions requiring specific macro discipline, e.g. "Azir needs to avoid early 2v2s," underperform in uncoordinated SQ). Recommended minimum sample: **~2,000 games** per champion for reliable win-rate signal; **Diamond II+** recommended as the SQ tier band most relevant to pro. **[Med — single source, but specific and mechanistically plausible]** — [itero.gg, "Can Solo Queue data be used for Professional play"](https://www.itero.gg/articles/sq-data), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Patch/meta drift**: the general gaming-analytics pattern (found in generic tracker/tier-list contexts, not LoL-pro-specific academic work) is **exponential decay weighting** — e.g., halving the influence of each prior week's data — to keep meta trackers responsive without being whipsawed by noise. No LoL-pro-specific study was found that rigorously derives an optimal half-life; this is an engineering choice rift-quant will need to backtest itself. **[Low-Med — generic pattern, not esports-pro-specific research]**

### 2.6 Blue side advantage

- **Aggregate, ~2020–2024, major leagues (LCK/LPL/LEC/LCS/MSI/Worlds), ~5,000 games**: Blue side **51.9%** overall; **52.8%** in Game 1 of a series specifically; **50.9%** in subsequent games. Yearly trend (declining): **2020: 52.1% → 2021: 51.8% → 2022: 51.5%**; but in 2022 *best-of series only* (excluding game 1), Red actually edged ahead slightly at **52.0%**, suggesting counter-drafting on red partially offsets blue's structural edge once a series is underway. **[Med]** — [Blue Side Advantage: A LoL Study](https://www.unrankedsmurfs.com/blog/lol-blue-side-advantage), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Worlds 2025** (first Worlds run fully under Fearless Draft): tournament-wide split was essentially **50/50 across 84 games**, with blue holding a slight edge in first tower/first Baron and red an edge in first dragon — but **LCK specifically went 58.3% on blue side**, credited to leveraging first-pick priority on high-value champions (K'Sante, Azir) to snowball. **Reading**: the average effect looks flat at the top level, but the edge is concentrated in teams that draft well enough to extract it — a "skill-dependent effect size" pattern worth modeling as an interaction (blue_side × team_draft_rating) rather than a flat coefficient. **[Med]** — esports.net / esportsheaven Worlds 2025 recaps, accessed 2026-08-21.
- **Mechanism**: blue picks first, forcing red into reactive bans around blue's power picks (one example cited: Lucian banned in 74 of 77 MSI games, 63 of those bans coming from red specifically). Red gets somewhat better dragon-pit approach angles/vision control; blue controls Baron-side positioning. Blue bot lane is *slightly* more likely to give up first blood (19.6% vs. red's 17.7%), a minor offsetting disadvantage. **[Med, single source]**
- **Fearless draft** (LPL first, 2024; expanded across majors through 2025; confirmed continuing through 2025) locks previously-used champions out for the rest of a series, raising champion diversity (Worlds 2025: 102 unique champions picked, ~30 kills/game average — called the "bloodiest Worlds ever") but multiple outlets (gameleap, blix.gg, esports.gg) explicitly flag it as **compounding** the blue-side pick-order advantage across a series, since red's counter-pick toolkit shrinks as champions get vaulted game-to-game. No single rigorous quantitative study isolating "fearless-draft-specific" blue-side delta was found — flagged as press-consensus, not a hard number. **[Med]**
- **Regime change — "First Selection" (2026 season, in effect now)**: Riot decoupled map-side choice from draft pick-order priority. The team with priority now picks **either** side **or** pick-order (not both); the opponent gets the other. This is an explicit, Riot-acknowledged fix for the structural blue-side edge. **This directly breaks stationarity of rift-quant's single strongest classical feature** — pre-2026 "blue side" data conflates two things (map side and pick priority) that are now independent variables. **[High — 8+ independently converging esports-press sources: egamersworld, invenglobal, vpesports, esports.gg, esportsinsider, gosugamers, escharts, sheepesports, totalapexentertainment]**, accessed 2026-08-21.

### 2.7 Momentum/tempo

- **General sports analytics**: the hot-hand debate remains genuinely contested — some recent work "vindicates" small hot-hand effects (e.g., a small NBA free-throw hot hand that "more than doubles" over longer streaks), other frameworks find the effect largely explained by statistical artifacts of streak-detection methodology. A 2025 tennis paper reframes momentum as a **latent "competitive state" via Hidden Markov Models**, claiming improved predictive calibration and transition stability over models using only observable features — i.e., momentum-as-latent-regime rather than momentum-as-raw-streak-feature may be the more defensible framing if rift-quant explores this. **[Med, general sports, not esports-specific]**
- **Esports-specific**: "Scalable Psychological Momentum Forecasting in Esports" (arXiv 2001.11274) models player-level psychological momentum/tilt as a learned temporal representation and claims that combining it with player-expertise features improves both pre-draft and post-draft win prediction. **Full-text verification was not achieved in this pass** (PDF extraction failed twice); the directional claim is corroborated by multiple independent search-engine summaries converging on the same characterization, but exact effect sizes remain unverified. **[Low-Med — directional claim only]**
- **Overall assessment for rift-quant**: momentum is not established as a robust, large, out-of-sample edge in esports specifically. Treat any momentum/streak feature (recent-series form, recent-game win/loss streak, in-game "tempo" swings) as **a hypothesis to backtest with strict out-of-sample validation**, not something to encode with a strong prior. **[This is our own synthesis, not a cited claim]**

### 2.8 Retrospective analysis: WPA, "deserved to win," throw detection

- **Win Probability Added (WPA)** is a mature, well-defined concept in traditional sports: the change in win probability attributable to a specific play/event, computed as win-prob-after minus win-prob-before. This transfers directly once an in-game win-probability model exists — define events (kill, objective secured, tower destroyed) and difference the model's own output across them. **[High, generic, directly portable]**
- **A production esports analog already exists**: HLTV's **Rating 3.0** for CS2 includes **"Round Swing,"** which measures the win-probability change attributable to each individual kill, contextualized by map, side, and economy state — i.e., WPA-for-esports, already shipped by the industry's leading stats site. This is strong evidence the approach is both feasible and valued by the esports-consuming public once you have it. **[Med-High]** — [HLTV, "Introducing Rating 3.0"](https://www.hltv.org/news/42485/introducing-rating-30), accessed 2026-08-21.
- **A LoL-specific coarse WPA table already exists implicitly** in the Samford role-based regression (§2.4/2.2): baron kill ≈ +24pp, ADC personal kill ≈ +5.9pp, blue side ≈ +7pp. This is a usable starting template for a first-pass event-value table before a full sequential model is built. **[Med]**
- **"Deserved to win" / xG-style framing**: unlike soccer's well-known "expected goals / deserved-to-win" concept, **no publicly available LoL-specific equivalent was found**. This looks like genuine whitespace: rift-quant would need to construct it itself, e.g., as (actual outcome) minus (model's own win-probability trajectory integrated over the game, or win-probability at a defined "neutral" checkpoint). **[Low confidence anything already exists — treat as a build-it-ourselves opportunity, not a literature gap to keep searching]**
- **Throw/collapse detection**: the literature that *does* exist under "esports anomaly detection" is about **match-fixing/fraud** (ensemble ML on betting-line-movement + performance-deviation features; one review reports ~80% accuracy for logistic regression/SVM and 92%+ for random forest/KNN/ensemble approaches) — a fraud-detection problem, not a skill/decision-error "throw" (collapsed a large lead) detector. **No LoL-specific, non-fraud "in-game collapse" detector analogous to NFL/NBA win-probability-crash analysis was found published.** The natural, directly buildable approach (flag games where model win-probability crossed a high threshold, e.g. >90%, and the team still lost) appears to be unclaimed territory. **[Med on the fraud-detection literature that does exist; Low/gap on genuine throw-detection]**

## 3. Recommended Model Architecture: v0 and v1

### v0 — fast, interpretable MVP (target: weeks, not months)

**Goal**: a defensible, well-calibrated baseline across the full draft-to-live spectrum, built almost entirely from well-worn, production-proven techniques found in this research (Oracle's Elixir-style LR, GD@15-style features, a simple Elo+region-strength rating).

- **Pre-game layer**: logistic regression / shallow GBM blend on:
  - Team rating: simple Elo with a region-strength additive term (`effective_rating = home_elo + region_strength`, region strength updated only on inter-region games) — directly mirrors both lolproelo.com's live implementation and the coarse structure of PandaSkill, without needing PandaSkill's full per-player performance-model machinery yet.
  - Blue-side dummy, **explicitly interacted with a "First Selection era" flag** (pre-2026 vs. 2026+), since these are not the same feature anymore (§2.6).
  - Draft signal, kept simple at v0: sum of patch-level champion win-rate deltas for picks minus bans (no synergy/counter matrix yet).
  - Recent form / head-to-head features (simple rolling win rate, last N games).
  - **Expected performance target**: pre-game accuracy in the **~58–63% range**, i.e., approaching but not necessarily beating the informally-reported ~63% bookmaker benchmark (§2.1) — appropriate for a v0 that hasn't yet modeled player-level skill or synergy.
- **In-game layer**: independent logistic regression models per time bucket (e.g., every 5 minutes, mirroring the Oracle's Elixir / classic-blog pattern) on gold diff, xp diff, kill diff, dragon/herald/baron/tower counts.
  - **Expected performance target**: tracking the triangulated literature curve — roughly 55–65% @10min, 65–75% @15–20min, 75–85% @25min, 90%+ @30–35min (§2.2). Treat any v0 number far outside this band as a red flag for a data/leakage bug before treating it as a win.
- **Calibration**: isotonic regression per time bucket where the held-out calibration set exceeds ~1,000 games; fall back to Platt scaling for sparser buckets (early pro-league data will likely force Platt in most buckets initially, given LoL pro leagues produce only on the order of a few thousand games/year across all four target leagues combined).
- **Why this is the right v0**: every component here matches what an actual deployed production system (Oracle's Elixir) or a converged independent implementation (lolproelo.com) already does — it is deliberately *not* novel, to establish a credible floor fast.

### v1 — richer, differentiated system (target: months)

- **Player rating layer**: adopt the **PandaSkill pattern directly** — per-role XGBoost "PScore" from box-score stats → percentile → OpenSkill Bayesian update run as Free-For-All (rank all 10 players by performance, not just team W/L) → dual contextual/meta rating decomposition for region calibration → σ-reset (not μ-reset) on roster/region changes. This is the single highest-leverage upgrade found in this research: it directly delivers the "per-player bias profile" rift-quant wants, naturally aggregates to a team rating, and gives us a published external benchmark to beat (**64.98–70.07%** pre-match accuracy, **67–70%** on inter-region games specifically).
- **Champion pool / matchup layer**: player-conditional champion strength via **mastery-prior shrinkage** (à la arXiv 2605.18338) — a pro player's rating on a given champion is a precision-weighted blend of (a) their own pro-game history on it, (b) their solo-queue history on it *adjusted by a champion-specific pro/SQ multiplier* (not a single global constant — §2.5), and (c) an archetype/embedding-cluster prior for near-zero-sample cases (new picks, role swaps).
- **Draft synergy/counter layer**: GAE-style pairwise embeddings (32-dim is a reasonable starting point, per the DGI graph paper's architecture) for synergy and counter, trained on a blended pro + high-elo solo-queue dataset, regularized toward zero for rare/unseen pairs, refreshed with **exponential patch-decay weighting** (half-life to be tuned empirically via backtest — no external LoL-specific study was found to borrow a validated value from, per §2.5).
- **In-game sequence layer**: upgrade from independent time-bucket LR to a **single GBM (XGBoost/LightGBM) with elapsed time as a raw feature plus explicit interaction terms** (e.g., gold_diff × spline(time)) — this pools statistical power across a data-scarce pro dataset and avoids discontinuities at bucket boundaries, at low implementation risk. Reserve LSTM/Transformer sequence modeling for **if/when** rift-quant obtains genuinely sequential/event-level live data (a real-time event feed, not just periodic snapshots) — per §2.3, that's the regime where sequence models have actually shown an edge in analogous titles (CS2, VALORANT), not the aggregated-snapshot regime most public LoL data comes in.
- **Fusion**: a meta-model (stacked logistic regression or a small GBM) blending pre-game rating output, draft synergy/counter output, and live in-game output, with the pre-game/draft components' influence allowed to decay toward the in-game model's output as game time increases — consistent with the empirically-observed pattern that gold-diff-type signals dominate and flatten out mid-to-late game regardless of pre-game priors.
- **Calibration**: isotonic per time-bucket as data volume allows, with a **mandatory recalibration cadence tied to patch releases** — since meta shifts (new patches) are a known, unaddressed source of silent miscalibration in everything reviewed here.
- **WPA / "deserved to win" / throw-flagging layer**: once the in-game model exists, this is close to a free byproduct — difference the model's own win-probability output across defined events for WPA-style attribution (matching the CS2 "Round Swing" pattern and extending the existing coarse LoL event-value table from §2.8), and flag "collapse" events as any game where model win probability crossed a high threshold (e.g. >90%) and the favored team still lost. **No existing public tool does this for LoL** — recommend treating it as a v1-stretch/v2-core deliverable and a plausible product/content differentiator, not just an internal QA tool.
- **Expected performance targets for v1**: pre-game accuracy in the **mid-to-high 60s%**, explicitly benchmarked against PandaSkill's 67–70% inter-region figure for Worlds/MSI-type matchups (our hardest and most commercially relevant case); in-game accuracy matching-or-beating the triangulated literature curve; calibration error (ECE) target **under ~1–2%**, benchmarked against the 0.57–1.11% range found in the one LoL-specific calibration paper (§2.3).

## 4. Open Questions / Risks

1. **Pro sample size is small and will remain the binding constraint.** A few thousand games/year across LCK+LPL+LEC+Americas is small for anything beyond fairly regularized models; heavy reliance on solo-queue transfer is necessary but, per §2.5, only reliable with champion-specific correction factors — a real overfitting risk if not handled carefully.
2. **Hodge et al.'s specific benchmark numbers could not be verified in this pass** — two automated extraction attempts on the PDF gave inconsistent results. Before citing any specific accuracy/Brier/calibration number from that paper (internally or externally), have someone read the primary text directly.
3. **2026's "First Selection" rule change breaks stationarity of the single strongest classical feature** (blue side). Pre-2026 and 2026+ data are arguably different regimes for this feature and should likely be modeled/flagged as such; there is also, by definition, only a partial season of 2026 data available as of this writing (2026-08-21) to characterize the new regime.
4. **Fearless draft's adoption has been staggered by league/year** (LPL 2024 first, broader rollout through 2025), so draft-phase dynamics (champion availability shrinking across a series) are themselves a moving target that needs an explicit regime/context flag rather than being pooled with pre-fearless-draft seasons.
5. **No external benchmark exists for the "deserved to win"/throw-detection layer** (§2.8) — this is upside (a differentiation opportunity) but also means there's no outside accuracy target to sanity-check v1's output against; validation will have to be manual/qualitative initially (do flagged "collapse" games actually match community/analyst consensus about a throw?).
6. **Momentum/tilt remains scientifically unsettled** even in traditional sports, and the one esports-specific paper found couldn't be fully verified in this pass. Any momentum feature should be added behind a strict backtested-lift gate, not on a strong prior that it must matter.
7. **Patch/meta-decay half-life has no validated external value to borrow** — this is a from-scratch empirical tuning task, not a literature lookup.
8. **Accuracy-number methodology traps are pervasive and easy to walk into by accident** (§2.1, §2.9 table) — pure draft vs. draft+identity, pro vs. solo-queue vs. mixed-rank, and random vs. time-based splits are all conflated across the ecosystem. rift-quant should fix and document its own eval protocol early, and always report which of these regimes a number belongs to — including when talking to outside parties (investors, counterparties for pricing contracts) about model performance.
9. **Data source landscape**: nearly everything reviewed here is built on either the Riot API directly or the community-standard Oracle's Elixir historical dataset; commercial live-data providers (e.g., GRID, Bayes Esports — not directly evaluated in this pass) were not investigated here and may be a gap worth a dedicated follow-up research task if not already covered elsewhere in the project's planning.
10. **Regional strength claims (LCK > LPL > LEC, etc.) are press-sourced snapshots**, not something to hard-code — they should be continuously re-derived from rift-quant's own inter-region rating layer (§2.4/v1) rather than assumed static.
