# Polymarket for LoL/Esports Quant Trading — Research Report

Compiled 2026-08-21. Scope: esports/LoL market structure, CLOB API mechanics, fees, onboarding, resolution, US/regulatory access, market making, and comparison data sources. All claims web-verified against 2025-2026 sources; confidence ratings and access dates given for load-bearing claims. This report describes publicly documented platform mechanics and legal/regulatory status only — it contains no guidance on evading geographic or regulatory restrictions.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Single-match LoL markets exist and are the norm, not just tournament winners.** Polymarket lists live moneyline (and spread/total) markets for individual LCK, LPL, LEC, LCS, CBLOL, LES, PRM and other regional matches, alongside tournament-level futures (Worlds, MSI, EWC). [High confidence]
- **Volume is real but thin and spiky.** A high-profile LPL/Worlds-adjacent match (Weibo Gaming vs. Invictus Gaming) hit **$3.8M in 24h volume**; a live LCK match sample on 2026-08-21 showed **$93K–$202K** per match, and a next-day match had only **~$1.9K**. Total "LoL Worlds" market volume was ~$681K in mid-2026, and a snapshot of active LoL markets showed ~93 markets totaling **$2.7M** combined volume. Liquidity is concentrated in marquee matchups/tournaments; regular-season weekday matches are noticeably thinner. [Med confidence — spot-check snapshots, not a volume time series]
- **Resolution is fast for esports specifically.** Polymarket resolves via the UMA Optimistic Oracle: undisputed outcomes finalize in **~2 hours**; disputed ones escalate to UMA's DVM taking **4-6 days**. Esports match resolution sources are the official league/publisher result (Riot/LEC for LoL) with Leaguepedia and "consensus of credible reporting" as backup — matches are typically confirmed within minutes of the game ending, and no major LoL-specific resolution dispute was found in research (general oracle disputes have occurred elsewhere, e.g., politics markets). [Med-High confidence]
- **Fees are no longer zero.** Polymarket rolled out taker fees in stages through 2026: **sports (incl. esports) launched at a 0.03 coefficient in March 2026 and rose to 0.05 in July 2026** (max ~$1.25 taker fee per 100 shares at the most price-sensitive point). **Makers pay 0% and receive a 15% rebate (sports) / 20% (crypto) of collected taker fees**, funded from the same fee pool, stacking with the separate Liquidity Rewards program. [High confidence]
- **CLOB API stack migrated to V2 on 2026-04-28**, breaking backward compatibility with V1. The original `py-clob-client` was **archived 2026-05-25** (read-only). Current path: `py-clob-client-v2` (actively maintained, e.g. v0.34.6-era releases) or the newer unified `py-sdk` (PyPI: `polymarket-client`, beta, last updated within days of this report, combines Gamma+Data+CLOB+WebSocket). Build against **v2 or py-sdk only**; do not start new work on the archived V1 client. [High confidence]
- **Onboarding is cheap and largely gasless.** Trading uses a Gnosis Safe proxy wallet on Polygon; Polymarket's relayer pays gas via meta-transactions. Deposit minimum is ~$2-3 USDC.e on Polygon (higher on other chains); practical starting capital is commonly cited as **$20-100**; minimum resting limit order is **5 shares**, minimum marketable (FOK/FAK) order is **$1 notional**. [High confidence]
- **US access is now legally possible but not for esports (yet), and not the same venue.** Polymarket acquired CFTC-licensed exchange **QCEX for $112M (July 2025)**, stood up **QCX LLC d/b/a "Polymarket US"** as a Designated Contract Market, and relaunched a US iOS app in **May 2026** (Android/web still pending as of this report). **Polymarket US's documented sports offering is traditional sports only (NBA/NFL/NHL/MLB/soccer/tennis/golf/UFC/cricket) — esports is not mentioned in its sports FAQ.** The original offshore Polymarket.com remains **geo-blocked for US persons**; using a VPN to evade this is a Terms-of-Service violation Polymarket actively enforces (IP/ASN blocking) and can lead to account termination and fund freezing. [High confidence on structure; Med on "esports not yet on Polymarket US" since it could change without a formal announcement]
- **A new, broad CFTC investigation into Polymarket opened around 2026-06-26** (marketing/promotional practices — fake trading videos, undisclosed paid influencers), separate from and after a prior CFTC/DOJ probe into US-person access that was **dropped without charges in July 2025**. This is a live regulatory overhang, not a resolved matter. [High confidence on existence; Med on eventual outcome]
- **Latency arbitrage against Polymarket's own esports/sports markets is a documented, named strategy, not theoretical.** Traders/bots (e.g., "TeemuTeemuTeemu") turned **~$900 into ~$208K over 3 months** trading LoL/Dota live markets by ingesting official game data faster than Polymarket's price reflects broadcast events (broadcast/stream delay commonly **15-45+ seconds**). A separate "reality arbitrage" wallet ("swisstony") reportedly turned **$5 into $3.7M** exploiting broadcast lag on traditional sports. This is the single most important structural fact for a prospective LoL/Polymarket strategy: **retail/broadcast-based signal is stale relative to on-chain price by tens of seconds**, and this edge is explicitly why fast data feeds (Riot's live client/game API, PandaScore frame/event WebSocket feeds) command a premium. [High confidence — multiple independent trade-press sources; specific PnL figures are Med confidence self-reported/on-chain-inferred numbers]
- **A recorder should be built now.** Given fee changes are recent (as of 2026), CLOB migrated versions (V1→V2→py-sdk) are in flux, and Polymarket's public API has **no historical order-book/price endpoint beyond `/prices-history`** (only live state otherwise), the compounding cost of *not* capturing tick-level LoL market data today is real — third-party data vendors (e.g., PolymarketData, Telonex) already sell what Polymarket doesn't provide for free, precisely because nobody archives it by default. See Recommendations.
- **Market making is documented as marginal-to-negative on illiquid/fast-moving books.** A public postmortem ("I cloned a Polymarket market-making bot") reported **no net profit** after execution bugs and adverse selection; a more sophisticated open-source bot (`poly-maker`) explicitly widens spreads/shrinks size using realized-vol and per-fill markout EWMAs because **"informed flow arrives too fast"** in illiquid sports/esports books — this is a direct, documented warning against naive two-sided quoting on LoL match books. [Med confidence — single-source anecdotes, but consistent with market microstructure theory]
- **Kalshi is a live, CFTC-regulated US alternative with materially larger esports flow**: **$36.18M esports volume across 408 matches** in one sample week (2026-06-01 to 06-07), rising to **$66.2M** the next week (IEM Cologne), with CS2 dominant (~66%) and LoL second (**$8.7M** across 116 matches in the sample week). Kalshi should be evaluated in parallel to Polymarket, especially for any US-based execution. [High confidence]
- **the-odds-api / PandaScore / Pinnacle are viable benchmark-odds sources**, but LoL-specific real-time odds/game-state data (frames/events feeds, sub-second) is a paid, esports-specialist product (PandaScore) rather than something bundled cheaply into general sportsbook-odds aggregators. [Med confidence on pricing specifics — vendor pricing pages change frequently]

**Category/fee classification.** Esports sits inside Polymarket's broader "Sports" fee tier (not a separate zero-fee tier); "Games" and "Esports" are UI subcategories of Sports sharing the same fee schedule. [High confidence, see §2.3]

### 2.2 API Mechanics

**Service map** (per [docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/introduction](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/introduction), fetched 2026-08-21):

| Service | Base URL | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Gamma API | `https://gamma-api.polymarket.com` | Market/event discovery & metadata (public, no auth) |
| CLOB API | `https://clob.polymarket.com` | Live order book, order placement/management, price history |
| Data API | `https://data-api.polymarket.com` | Positions, trades, account/market analytics |
| Relayer API | `https://relayer-v2.polymarket.com` | Gasless transaction submission (meta-tx) |
| Bridge API | `https://bridge.polymarket.com` | Deposits/withdrawals |
| WebSocket | (per-service) | Real-time market, account, sports, RFQ streams |

**Gamma API for discovery:** public, no API key required. Key endpoints are `/markets` and `/events` (an event groups 1+ markets), plus `/public-search?q=` for free-text discovery across markets/events/profiles. Pagination via `limit` (max 500) + `offset`; must loop until a short page is returned to enumerate the full universe. ([dlthub.com/context/source/polymarket-gamma](https://dlthub.com/context/source/polymarket-gamma), [chainstack.com/polymarket-api-for-developers](https://chainstack.com/polymarket-api-for-developers/)) [High confidence]

**CLOB order types** (per [docs.polymarket.com/trading/orders/overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/orders/overview) and corroborating trade-press):
- **GTC** (Good-Till-Cancelled) — default, rests until filled/cancelled.
- **GTD** (Good-Till-Date) — expires at a set `expiresAt` timestamp.
- **FOK** (Fill-Or-Kill) — fills completely and immediately or is cancelled.
- **FAK** (Fill-And-Kill, i.e. IOC) — fills what it can immediately, cancels remainder.
- **Post-only** can be combined with GTC/GTD but *not* with FOK/FAK.

**Tick size / order size** (third-party corroborated; official docs did not spell out exact numbers in the fetched page, so treat as Med confidence pending direct verification against a live market's `/book` response):
- Tick sizes: **0.1, 0.01, 0.001, or 0.0001**, shrinking dynamically as price approaches the extremes (price **> 0.96** or **< 0.04**).
- Minimum **marketable** order (FOK/FAK, or a BUY that crosses the book): **$1 notional**.
- Minimum **resting** GTC/GTD limit order: **5 shares** (no notional floor beyond that).
- FAK/FOK precision constraints: sell orders to 2 decimal places, taker amount to 4 decimals, size×price product ≤ 2 decimals.

Sources: [Polymarket agent-skills — order-patterns.md](https://github.com/Polymarket/agent-skills/blob/main/order-patterns.md), [nautilustrader.io Polymarket integration docs](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/integrations/polymarket/), [tradingvps.io order types guide](https://tradingvps.io/polymarket-order-types-explained/). [Med-High confidence]

**negRisk (multi-outcome) markets:** Used for mutually-exclusive multi-outcome events (e.g., "Worlds 2026 Winning Region," a tournament winner market), *not* typically for a 2-way single-match moneyline. Mechanics: only one outcome can resolve Yes; summed Yes prices ≈ $1.00; a No token on outcome A converts 1:1 into Yes tokens on every other outcome; this collapses the collateral needed to hold "buy every outcome" positions from the naive sum-of-prices down to 1.0 collateral unit. **Relevant to any strategy trading Worlds/MSI tournament-winner markets (arbitrage across the region-winner book), not to weekly single-match trading.** ([docs.polymarket.com/advanced/neg-risk](https://docs.polymarket.com/advanced/neg-risk), [bucko.ai negRisk guide](https://www.bucko.ai/learn/polymarket-negative-risk-markets-guide)) [High confidence]

**Rate limits** (per [docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/rate-limits](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/rate-limits), fetched 2026-08-21 — treat these exact figures as High confidence, direct from official docs):

| Endpoint class | Limit |
|---|---|
| Gamma general | 4,000 req / 10s |
| Gamma `/events` | 500 req / 10s |
| Gamma `/markets` | 300 req / 10s |
| Gamma `/public-search` | 350 req / 10s |
| Data API general | 1,000 req / 10s |
| Data API `/trades` | 200 req / 10s |

WebSocket subscription caps are **not officially published**; a commonly cited self-imposed conservative client-side bound is **200 subscriptions/connection**, but this is not a venue-enforced limit per available sources. **Recommendation: use WebSocket for real-time book/trade data, REST only for one-shot lookups and order placement** — this is Polymarket's own stated guidance. [Med-High confidence]

**Historical data gap:** The public CLOB API's only historical endpoint is **`/prices-history`** (timestamp + price series, no key required). There is **no public historical order-book (L2) or full trade-tape endpoint** — Data API `/trades` is scoped to a user or market's recent activity, not a bulk historical export. This gap is filled commercially by third parties (PolymarketData, Telonex) selling "L2 books from August 2025 onward," which is itself evidence that **nobody gets this data for free once market conditions have passed** — i.e., you cannot backfill esports order-book history later; it must be captured live. ([polymarketdata.co](https://www.polymarketdata.co/polymarket-historical-data), [telonex.io](https://telonex.io/)) [Med confidence on exact gap boundaries, High confidence on the general "no free L2 history" conclusion]

**Client libraries — current state (important, moved fast in 2026):**
1. **`py-clob-client` (V1)** — **archived 2026-05-25**, read-only, last release v0.34.6 (2026-02-19). **Do not build on this.**
2. **`py-clob-client-v2`** — official V2 client; matches the **CLOB V2 migration that went live 2026-04-28** (no backward compatibility with V1 — a V1 integration simply stops working against production). L1/L2 auth mechanics unchanged from V1 (existing API key/secret/passphrase still valid). Actively maintained (68 commits, 61 open issues per GitHub at fetch time) but its own README now points to something newer.
3. **`py-sdk`** (PyPI: `polymarket-client`) — the **new unified SDK** ("Unified Python SDK for Polymarket DeFi"), folding Gamma + Data + CLOB + WebSocket into one package; **beta**, 98 stars/21 forks, **last updated 2026-08-13** (i.e., within the last two weeks of this report). Polymarket's own docs/READMEs now recommend this for **new projects**.

**Practical recommendation for rift-quant:** start on **`py-sdk`** for new development given it's the actively-developed, officially-recommended path and unifies discovery+trading+streaming; fall back to `py-clob-client-v2` only if `py-sdk`'s beta status causes instability, and treat V1 as dead. [High confidence on the lineage/dates; Med on which to pick long-term since py-sdk is beta and could still change]

### 2.3 Fees and Incentives (2025-2026)

Polymarket was historically **zero-fee** for both makers and takers. **This changed in 2026.** Timeline:
- **January 2026:** fee rollout begins on high-frequency crypto markets.
- **February 2026:** rollout extends to select sports markets.
- **March 2026:** sports fee coefficient launches at **0.03**.
- **~March 30, 2026:** broader schedule in effect — crypto 0.07, sports 0.03, finance/politics/mentions/tech 0.04, economics/culture/weather/other 0.05; geopolitics/world-events markets remain fee-free.
- **July 2026:** sports coefficient raised **0.03 → 0.05**, lifting max taker fee from **$0.75 to $1.25 per 100 shares** at the fee-maximizing price point.

**Fee formula:** `fee = shares × category_rate × price × (1 − price)` — i.e., fees are **maximized at p=0.50** (max uncertainty) and taper to near-zero near 0 or 1. This is a standard "fee on variance" design and is directly relevant to modeling: a LoL moneyline sitting near a coinflip (as many are, pre-game) pays close to the *maximum* per-category fee, while a heavy favorite/underdog line near 0.90/0.10 is nearly fee-free to take.

**Maker treatment:** Makers (resting limit orders that get filled passively) pay **0% fee** in all cases and additionally receive a **daily rebate**: **15% of collected taker fees in Sports, 20% in Crypto**, paid out from the fee pool. This **stacks** with the separate **Liquidity Rewards program** (below) — i.e., a market maker on a LoL book can earn (a) the spread, (b) a rebate share of taker fees, and (c) liquidity-rewards payouts simultaneously, but conversely a *directional taker* (someone crossing the spread to bet on a match) pays the full fee.

**Liquidity Rewards Program:** separate from the maker-rebate share. Pays makers daily (midnight UTC) based on order size and closeness to the market's average/mid price; a day only pays out once cumulative eligible earnings reach **$1**. Eligibility requires minimum size, maximum spread, and minimum live-duration thresholds that Polymarket does not fully publish numerically in the fetched docs. Recent example: Polymarket added **$1M in extra liquidity rewards** (through August 2026) targeted at crypto 5-min/15-min/4-hour TWAP-settled markets specifically — i.e., **incentive emphasis has recently skewed toward crypto microstructure markets, not sports/esports**, which is a signal that esports market-making rewards may be comparatively less rich right now. [Med confidence on the relative emphasis claim]

Sources: [help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364478-trading-fees](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364478-trading-fees), [docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards), [igamingbusiness.com — Polymarket sports fee hike](https://igamingbusiness.com/prediction-markets/polymarket-sports-fee-hike-2026/), [startpolymarket.com/learn/polymarket-fees](https://startpolymarket.com/learn/polymarket-fees/), [kucoin.com/blog/polymarket-fees-trading-guide-2026](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/polymarket-fees-trading-guide-2026). [High confidence on fee existence and category rates; Med on precise reward-program numeric thresholds]

### 2.4 Onboarding

**Wallet architecture:** On first trade, Polymarket deploys a **Gnosis Safe proxy wallet** on Polygon, single-signer (you), holding your USDC.e and positions. Benefits: gasless trading (Polymarket's relayer submits and pays for transactions via meta-tx/account abstraction), atomic multi-step execution, and pre-set allowances to the Exchange contract (no per-trade approval popups). Login is via MetaMask/EOA or embedded wallet (Magic-based email login for non-crypto-native users — see `github.com/Polymarket/magic-proxy-builder-example`). [High confidence]

**Funding:** Polymarket only accepts **USDC on Polygon** (labeled USDC.e in older UI/discussions — verify current UI labeling at trade time since USDC vs. USDC.e bridging has shifted across 2025-2026). Sending USDC from Ethereum mainnet directly will **not** credit your account — the network must be explicitly set to Polygon when withdrawing from an exchange (Coinbase/Kraken/Binance all support this). Minimum deposit is roughly **$2-3** on most chains (higher, ~$7-10, if funding via Ethereum or BTC/USDT directly, reflecting L1 gas/bridging overhead baked into the minimum). [High confidence]

**Practical starting capital:** No hard platform-enforced minimum beyond the deposit floor; commonly cited practical starting range for exploring markets is **$20-100** (to absorb on-ramp/card fees and trade across a few positions); contract prices range $0.01-$0.99 so per-share cost is trivially small. For a systematic strategy this is not the binding constraint — book depth and fee drag at your target trade size will matter far more than the account minimum. [Med confidence — these are retail-education figures, not rigorous quant sizing]

**API key derivation (L1/L2 auth):**
- **L1:** EIP-712 signature from your wallet's private key, used once to derive/authorize L2 credentials ("L1-to-L2 authentication").
- **L2:** Every authenticated CLOB request is signed with **HMAC-SHA256** over `timestamp_seconds + METHOD + path + body`, using the L1-derived secret. The derivation response yields three values used going forward: `apiKey`, `secret`, `passphrase`.
- Confirmed unchanged across the V1→V2 CLOB migration (existing credentials continue to work).

Sources: [github.com/Polymarket/agent-skills/blob/main/authentication.md](https://github.com/Polymarket/agent-skills/blob/main/authentication.md), [docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/authentication](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/authentication). [High confidence]

**Gas costs:** Effectively **near-zero for the trader** because Polymarket's relayer/meta-transaction system absorbs it; underlying Polygon gas itself runs **~$0.003-$0.01/tx** (down from ~$0.01-0.05 historically per some sources — figures vary by source and network conditions, but all agree it's functionally negligible next to Ethereum L1's $10-50/tx). You generally do not need to hold POL/MATIC yourself for normal trading. [High confidence on "gas is not a practical barrier"]

### 2.5 Resolution Mechanics

**Oracle:** Polymarket resolution runs on **UMA's Optimistic Oracle** (`uma-ctf-adapter` contract). Mechanism: an automated/permissioned proposer posts an outcome; anyone can dispute during a liveness window by posting a bond; if undisputed, the outcome finalizes; if disputed, a second round occurs, and if *that* is also disputed, resolution escalates to **UMA's DVM** (a token-holder vote). Polymarket runs **automated proposers** that handle the vast majority of resolutions without human intervention. ([docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution), [github.com/Polymarket/uma-ctf-adapter](https://github.com/Polymarket/uma-ctf-adapter)) [High confidence]

**Timeline:** **~2 hours** to finalize when undisputed; **4-6 days total** when disputed and escalated to DVM. For esports specifically, matches "typically confirmed within minutes" of ending per trade-press summaries, consistent with the automated-proposer / 2-hour undisputed path being the norm for a well-defined "who won the game" outcome. [Med-High confidence — the 2hr/4-6day figures are well-corroborated for the platform generally; esports-specific timing is inferred/summarized rather than directly measured from a match-level audit]

**LoL resolution source of truth:** Primary source is the **official league/publisher result** — Riot Games / the relevant regional league (LEC, LCK, etc.) — with **Leaguepedia (lol.fandom.com)** cited as a supporting reference and "consensus of credible reporting" as a fallback where official sources lag. This mirrors the general Polymarket pattern for esports (Valve as source-of-truth for Dota/CS, Riot for LoL). [Med confidence — this is a synthesized trade-press description, not a direct quote of Polymarket's formal UMA resolution-criteria text for a specific market, which should be checked per-market before relying on it]

**Dispute risk — general platform context (no LoL-specific incident found):** Research surfaced **no documented LoL-specific resolution controversy**. However, the *general* UMA/Polymarket resolution process has had real, documented problems elsewhere on the platform that are relevant as base-rate risk:
- A **Wall Street Journal investigation** found that in disputed markets, **~20% of outcomes involved judges/proposers with a financial stake**, and **~60% of judges were directly linked to Polymarket trading accounts** — a structural conflict-of-interest finding.
- At least one high-profile market (a purported US-Ukraine minerals deal) resolved **"Yes" despite no deal actually being signed**, illustrating that "resolves on the spirit of the market" interpretation can diverge from ground truth.
- 2025 disputes on Ukraine/Zelenskyy-related markets drew accusations that large token holders ("whales") used outsized DVM voting power to sway contested resolutions.

**Implication for LoL trading:** clean, binary, quickly-verifiable outcomes (which "who won this LoL game" mostly is) sit in the *lowest-risk* category of Polymarket's resolution surface — but the platform-wide governance/conflict-of-interest issues mean **you should not assume zero tail-risk on resolution**, especially for a market with any rules ambiguity (e.g., a match restarted after a bug/DC, a forfeit, a best-of-series map-count edge case). Read the specific market's resolution criteria text before sizing a position near an ambiguous edge case. [Med confidence]

### 2.7 Market Making: Mechanics, Adverse Selection, Latency

**How MMs operate on Polymarket:** Standard CLOB market making — resting bid/ask limit orders on both sides, earning the spread on round-trip fills, funded/incentivized by the stacked maker-rebate (§2.3) + Liquidity Rewards (§2.3) programs on top of raw spread capture. Two public, inspectable examples of real implementations:
- **`warproxxx/poly-maker`** (GitHub) — an automated two-sided MM bot that explicitly models **adverse selection via per-fill "markout" EWMAs** (i.e., tracks how the price moves *against* the bot immediately after each fill) combined with **realized-volatility EWMAs**, using both to **dynamically widen spreads and shrink quoted size** in markets that are "picking the bot off." This is a direct, code-level acknowledgment that naive constant-spread quoting loses money on markets with fast-moving informed flow.
- **A Substack postmortem** ("I cloned a Polymarket market-making bot and ran it") reported the author made **no net profit overall** after execution bugs and adverse selection ate the theoretical spread income — a useful real-world data point that a plug-and-play MM bot is not free money even before considering the strategy's alpha (or lack thereof).
- Commentary from the same research thread frames it sharply: **"politics binaries sit in the makeable region where spread exceeds adverse-selection cost, while crypto event markets and illiquid sports books are above the break-even line because informed flow arrives too fast."** This is a direct, on-point warning for LoL: **thin, fast-moving live in-game markets are structurally the *hardest* place to market-make profitably**, not the easiest, precisely because of the latency-arb dynamic in the next paragraph.

Sources: [github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker), [tezlee.substack.com — I cloned a Polymarket market-making bot](https://tezlee.substack.com/p/i-cloned-a-polymarket-market-making), [github.com/sonnyfully/polymarket-bot](https://github.com/sonnyfully/polymarket-bot). [Med confidence — small sample of public writeups, but internally consistent and consistent with market-microstructure theory]

**Latency vs. broadcast — the central structural fact for LoL specifically:** Multiple independent trade-press pieces (2025-2026) document **named, profitable latency-arbitrage strategies against Polymarket's own live esports/sports markets**:
- **"TeemuTeemuTeemu"** — turned **~$900 → ~$208,521 profit over 3 months**, trading almost exclusively **live LoL and Dota 2** markets, by "continuously monitoring live games, ingesting official match data and parsing broadcast information faster than the market can react" — i.e., entering on kills/objectives/teamfights before the Polymarket price updates, exiting once it does. Largest single win: **~$90K on a Dplus KIA vs. Hanwha Life Esports** match. ([esports.net](https://www.esports.net/news/polymarket-bot-makes-over-200k-in-3-months-with-lol-dota-2-esports/))
- **"swisstony"** — a **general reality-arbitrage** wallet (traditional sports, not esports-specific) that turned **$5 → $3.7M**, exploiting the **15-40 second** gap between live stadium events and TV broadcast by ingesting stadium data feeds directly. Framed by commentators as **"a lag tax on your TV broadcast."** ([phemex.com](https://phemex.com/news/article/algorithm-exploits-broadcast-lag-to-turn-5-into-37m-on-polymarket-53969))
- General latency figures cited: **Twitch/streaming broadcast delay ~15-45 seconds**; official esports **game-state/data APIs can update within 1-2 seconds** of an in-game event (round end, kill, objective). That gap (**tens of seconds**) is the exploitable window.
- A second, non-bot trader (**"xdd07070"**) made **$118,748 profit over 1 month** (90% win rate, 36-4) trading LoL/Dota/CS/Valorant matches with **manual, discretionary "strong favorite" picks** (entering at >55c) rather than a latency bot — evidence that this market also rewards simple disciplined favorite-backing, separate from the pure-speed strategy.
- **Direct implication for rift-quant:** if the win-probability model's edge is meant to come from *modeling* (better calibrated win-probability given draft/gold/objectives state) rather than *speed*, it will be competing in a market where **speed-based edge is already being harvested by named, profitable bots reading official game data feeds** (e.g., Riot's live client/spectator data or PandaScore's frame/event WebSocket feed, sub-second). A model-only (non-latency) strategy should expect the **easy, mechanical "kill happened, price hasn't moved yet" edge to already be competed away** in liquid matches, and should instead target **either (a) genuine probability-model edge beyond what fast-but-simple event-parsing bots capture (e.g., better handling of gold/XP diffs, comeback dynamics, team-specific tendencies), or (b) the thinner regular-season matches where latency bots may not bother to operate** (lower volume = lower payoff for infrastructure investment).

## 3. Recommendations

1. **Build an odds/order-book recorder now — before the modeling work is even finished.** Rationale: (a) Polymarket's public API has **no free historical L2 order-book endpoint** — third parties already charge for exactly this, meaning the data genuinely disappears once live; (b) the fee schedule, CLOB client version, and even market category boundaries (esports classification, Polymarket US scope) are all **actively moving in 2026** — capturing ground truth now is cheaper than reconstructing it later; (c) marquee-vs-thin volume bimodality (§2.1) and the latency-arb dynamic (§2.7) are both things your model needs **your own tick data** to characterize, not third-party summaries. Minimum viable recorder: poll/subscribe to Gamma `/events` for LoL market discovery + CLOB WebSocket (or `/book` polling within rate limits) for every open LoL market's order book and trades, timestamped, stored append-only. This is a small, well-scoped build (a few hundred lines), not a big lift, and every day of delay is unrecoverable data.
2. **Stand up the same recorder against Kalshi and at least one benchmark odds feed (Pinnacle via an aggregator, or PandaScore) in parallel.** You want three synchronized time series per match — Polymarket price, Kalshi price, "sharp" reference odds — to (a) measure Polymarket-specific mispricing vs. a sharper market, and (b) directly quantify the broadcast-lag/latency-arb window described in §2.7 rather than relying on trade-press anecdotes.
3. **Build on `py-sdk` (or `py-clob-client-v2` as fallback), never the archived V1 client.** Given the API surface changed materially in the last 4 months (V2 migration, new unified SDK), pin dependency versions and re-check the Polymarket GitHub org monthly during active development — this ecosystem is not stable yet.
4. **Do not plan around Polymarket US for esports.** Its documented sports scope is traditional sports only as of this report; if US-person legal compliance is a hard requirement, evaluate **Kalshi** (already has CFTC-blessed esports contracts and larger measured LoL/esports flow) as the primary US-compliant execution venue, and treat offshore Polymarket.com access as a separate legal question to resolve explicitly (not by default) given active geo-enforcement and a live CFTC investigation.
5. **Model fees into any backtest/PnL simulation from day one**, not as an afterthought — the sports taker-fee coefficient already doubled once in 2026 (0.03→0.05) and fee formula peaks exactly at the p=0.5 region where most pre-game LoL moneylines sit. A strategy that looked profitable on a zero-fee historical assumption may not be under current (or future, likely-rising) fees.
6. **Treat naive two-sided market making on live/in-game LoL books as a likely loser**, per the documented adverse-selection experience (§2.7) — if pursuing MM at all, start on pre-game (not live in-game) LoL moneylines where price moves are slower, and explicitly implement markout/realized-vol based spread-widening (as `poly-maker` does) rather than constant-spread quoting.
7. **Re-verify the exact tick-size/min-order-size numbers directly against a live market's CLOB response** before finalizing execution-sizing logic — the figures in §2.2 are Med-confidence (third-party corroborated, not confirmed word-for-word in official docs at fetch time).

## 4. Open Questions / Risks

**Regulatory:**
- Outcome of the **June 2026 CFTC investigation** into Polymarket's marketing practices is unresolved; could result in fines, forced disclosure changes, or (less likely but not zero) broader operating restrictions that spill over into the CFTC's willingness to approve Polymarket US's pending April 2026 application for expanded US trading.
- Whether/when **esports markets appear on Polymarket US** is unknown — worth a standing watch item, not a one-time check, since the US product surface (Combos launched Aug 19, 2026) is evolving quickly.
- Geo-restriction list is **expanding, not stable** (30+ countries as of August 2026, several added recently) — any assumption about "where this is legal to use" has a short shelf life and should be re-checked at execution time, not assumed from this report.
- Minnesota's prediction-market ban (effective Aug 1, 2026) may be a leading indicator of more US state-level action even under the federal CFTC-preemption framework the Third Circuit endorsed — the preemption question itself may still be appealed (only one circuit has ruled).

**Adverse selection / latency:**
- The core open question for rift-quant's edge thesis: **is there durable alpha in win-probability *modeling* once fast event-parsing bots have already captured the mechanical "price hasn't updated yet" edge?** This report found strong evidence the mechanical edge exists and is being harvested (§2.7), but no evidence either way on how much *modeling*-based edge (as opposed to *speed*-based edge) remains on top of it. This needs to be answered empirically with your own recorder + backtest, not assumed.
- Unquantified: exactly how much of Polymarket LoL order flow today is latency-bot vs. discretionary vs. uninformed — this determines whether resting maker orders in live LoL markets are safe or a magnet for adverse selection.

**Liquidity:**
- Volume figures in this report are **spot snapshots**, not a time series — real capacity (how much size can be traded without moving the market X%) for a "typical" (non-marquee) LCK/LPL/LEC match is **unknown** and should be the first thing measured once the recorder is live.
- The gap between Kalshi's measured weekly LoL flow (~$8.7M/week in one sample) and Polymarket's LoL vertical snapshot (~$2.7M total across all open markets) is suggestive but **not a controlled comparison** — needs same-window, same-methodology measurement before concluding Kalshi > Polymarket for LoL liquidity specifically.
- Esports Liquidity Rewards program details (exact minimum size/max spread/duration thresholds) were **not published in the fetched docs** — needed before assuming MM rewards make esports market-making viable.
